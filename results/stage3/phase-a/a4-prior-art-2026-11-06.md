## The sentence that survives prior art

> **Against review grounded in the deployer's task stream, whether splitting a harm across AI agents pays is decided by cover, meaning how densely honest tasks license each part, and by the level of a deployer → planner → executor task hierarchy the monitor grounds to. Every monitor and single-agent defence checked here fixes that level at one trusted task. This programme treats it as a design variable, against an attacker who can write the subtask that licenses its own edit.**

**Already prior art, so each must be credited, not claimed:**

| idea | prior art |
|---|---|
| the monitor sees the agent's task | AI Control, SHADE-Arena, Ctrl-Z, Wen et al., FakeLab, Hills et al. |
| what the monitor knows sets the FPR it needs | FakeLab: 3.6 % with the full task, 7.8 % with a one-line summary, 13.4 % with summary plus distractor; Hills et al.'s history and state variants |
| distributed attacks evade per-unit monitors | FakeLab, Hills et al., Brown et al. |
| stateful, cross-context monitoring helps | Brown et al.; Hills et al.'s link-tracker; the CSA note |
| grouping with unknown membership inside benign noise | Ning et al.; HOLMES |
| a maximum-over-groups score calibrated against a Monte Carlo null | Turnbull et al. 1990, credited by Kulldorff 1997; Neill 2012 |
| grounding single-agent actions in the user's task | Task Shield, Conseca, Progent, CaMeL |
| an attacker that writes its own licence, in part | Progent Appendix E: an injected prompt steers the policy updater |

**Nearest precedents to the level variable, to cite and distinguish:**
- Task Shield Appendix A.2 names system-level "hierarchical alignment" but fixes the user level. Its §3.2 lets subtasks from tool outputs pass if they serve a user goal, which is the opening for laundering, not studied there.
- Conseca §3.1 names a precision-against-compromise tradeoff in which context to trust (context, not task level; not varied).
- CaMeL §8 discusses global policies above a rogue user (discussion only).
- In Wen et al.'s plan-based micro-protocols, the trusted model scores the subtasks of the untrusted model's own plan.

## Corrections to the proposal's text, for the paper

1. **§1 item 8 and §2 (FakeLab):**
   - Replace "a specificity curve" with "Figure 5 and Table 12 (App. D.9): the GPT-5 mini monitor needs FPR 3.6 % (full task), 7.8 % (one-line summary) or 13.4 % (summary plus distractor) to hold undetected success at or under 1 % with 95 % posterior confidence".
   - Replace the mediation with "cover story 53 % against dilution 25 % of the per-commit drop at K = 9 (45 % against 20 % for the peak commit); about 40 % unexplained; observational (Table 1, §3.1.1)".
   - Name the distractor variant `shortened_1_distractor`, and note that `none` is code-only.
   - Carry the joint monitor's caveats: non-deployable, full-task prompt only, partial coverage.
2. **§2 (Hills):** cite v2. The figures are the averages of the CLI and Flask families (Table 7). Use the paper's own history sentence ("more raw history does not improve detection"). The task-description monitor sees all completed task descriptions plus the current diff.
3. **§2 (Brown):** replace the figures with "at 10× benign background, 81 to 87 % recall at 2.4 to 3.1 % FPR against 75 % at 2.7 % for the per-context monitor; at 100×, 66 % against 55 % at 1.5 %". Replace "its parts cluster because they are unlike benign traffic" with the paper's mechanism: subtasks share features inherited from the exploit objective, recoverable by embedding. Add that its monitors judge against a policy, with no task.
4. **§2 (CSA):** cite by its title and date. It is a secondary, AI-assisted note: never a source for numbers.
5. **§2 ("monitors see the task by default"):** true for AI Control, SHADE-Arena (through the transcript's user message), Ctrl-Z (trusted monitor), Wen et al., FakeLab and Hills. **Brown et al. is the exception** (policy, no task). Every one grounds at a single task level.
6. **§2 (task-alignment defences):** reword to "single-agent defences ground the agent's actions in the user's task, either by checking each action against goals extracted from the user's messages (Task Shield) or by deriving least-privilege tool policies (Conseca; Progent v3) or a control-flow plan with provenance-tracking capabilities (CaMeL) from the trusted query; all four treat the user's task as the trusted root."
7. **§2 (grouping):** "None of it is an LLM judgement of natural-language composition with a calibrated maximum-over-subsets null" overclaims. The calibration is scan statistics' (Turnbull et al. 1990; Kulldorff 1997; Neill 2012). What stays new is that the group score is an LLM's judgement of natural-language composition, which is neither additive nor a likelihood ratio. So exact fast search does not apply, and the null must be rebuilt by replaying the search-and-judge pipeline on honest replicas (argued).
8. **Not yet checked; check before claiming "natural language" or "LLM" as untouched ground for scan statistics.** Seen in search excerpts only:
   - the semantic scan statistic over free-text complaints (Neill lab, 2014);
   - subset scanning over neural-network activations (Speakman et al., arXiv 1810.08676);
   - subset scanning over generative-model activations (Cintas et al., IJCAI 2022).
9. **The headline's first clause** ("limited by what the monitor is allowed to know"). FakeLab's Table 12 already shows the required FPR rising as task information is withdrawn, so the clause must credit FakeLab. The new part is the hierarchy level and cover, not the fact that information matters.
